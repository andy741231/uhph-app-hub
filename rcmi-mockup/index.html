Why We Exist

We turn research into better health for Houston and beyond.

The Research Centers in Minority Institutions (RCMI) program at the University of Houston builds lasting research capacity, develops the next generation of investigators, and partners directly with communities to reduce the burden of chronic disease.

Research Capacity

We build the infrastructure, tools, and expertise that make rigorous chronic-disease research possible.

Investigator Development

We mentor early-stage investigators, fund pilot projects, and clear the path from idea to impact.

Community Partnership

We work side-by-side with communities so research reflects real priorities and produces real-world change.

The Investigator Journey

From “I have an idea” to real-world impact.

Every step links directly to the service that moves you forward. Hover any step to see where it leads.

  1. I have an idea

    Start with a research question about chronic disease in your community.

  2. Need collaborators

    Connect with clinicians, biostatisticians, and community partners.

  3. Need consultation

    Get study design, methods, and navigation support from the Research Capacity Core.

  4. Need pilot funding

    Apply for pilot awards through the Investigator Development Core.

  5. Need community partners

    Engage organizations through the Community Engagement Core.

  6. Need implementation support

    Translate findings into practice with implementation science expertise.

  7. Need dissemination

    Publish, present, and share results with both academic and community audiences.

  8. Need evaluation

    Measure impact and feed lessons back into the next research question.

Research in Action

Stories that answer: so what?

Each story follows the same structure — what problem, what we learned, why it matters, what's next.

Coaching Diabetes in East Houston

What problem?
Uncontrolled type 2 diabetes in a low-resource neighborhood.
What did we learn?
Community health coaches reduced A1c by 1.4 points on average.
Why does it matter?
It's a scalable, low-cost model that works where clinics can't reach.
What's next?
Expanding to three additional ZIP codes in 2026.

Implementing Blood-Pressure Pathways

What problem?
Hypertension control lagged in community clinics.
What did we learn?
Standardized pathways plus weekly data review improved control by 18%.
Why does it matters?
It shows implementation science can move the needle in real clinics.
What's next?
Packaging the pathway for statewide adoption.

Listening to Older Adults

What problem?
Chronic disease isolation among seniors in Fort Bend County.
What did we learn?
Peer-led discussion groups reduced loneliness and improved self-management.
Why does it matter?
It reframes isolation as a treatable chronic-disease risk factor.
What's next?
Training 20 new peer leaders across the region.
