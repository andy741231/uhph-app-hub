A Manifesto · Not an About Page

Why
We
Exist.

The Research Centers in Minority Institutions (RCMI) program at the University of Houston exists for one reason: to turn research into better health for Houston and beyond. This is the short version.

Chronic disease doesn't spread evenly. It clusters in neighborhoods, follows historical disinvestment, and thrives where research infrastructure is thin. Houston — the most diverse city in America — is also a city where life expectancy can vary by twenty years between ZIP codes five miles apart.

The RCMI at the University of Houston exists to close that gap. Not with a single study, but by building three things at once: the research capacity to do rigorous work, the investigator development to grow the people who do it, and the community partnerships that make sure the work matters.

"We don't study communities. We work with them. The research question comes from the neighborhood, not the spreadsheet." — RCMI Principle

That's the whole pitch. Everything else — the four cores, the investigator journey, the dashboard, the stories, the publications — is just how we do those three things. This site is organized around the work, not the org chart.

If you have an idea, we have a path. If you have a community, we have researchers. If you have funding, we have impact. Start here.

The Three Things

What we build

  • Research Capacity — infrastructure, tools, methods, and expertise that make rigorous chronic-disease research possible.
  • Investigator Development — mentoring, pilot funding, and career pathways for the next generation of researchers.
  • Community Partnership — side-by-side work with organizations so research reflects real priorities and produces real change.
Funded by

NIH Research Centers in Minority Institutions program, hosted at the University of Houston.
